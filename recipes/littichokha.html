Litti Chokha

Description

Litti Chokha is a famous rustic dish from Bihar, Jharkhand, and Eastern Uttar Pradesh consisting of spiced, sattu-stuffed whole-wheat balls served with a smoky roasted vegetable mash.

Ingredients

For the Litti Dough (Outer Shell)

  • Whole wheat flour (Atta): 2 cups
  • Ghee: 2 tablespoons (for binding/moyan)
  • Carom seeds (Ajwain): ½ teaspoon (crushed between palms)
  • Salt: ½ teaspoon
  • Water: As needed to knead a firm dough

For the Sattu Stuffing (Inside the Litti)

  • Sattu (Roasted gram flour): 1 cup
  • Onion: 1 medium, finely chopped
  • Garlic: 5-6 cloves, finely minced
  • Ginger: 1-inch piece, grated or finely minced
  • Green chilies: 2-3, finely chopped
  • Fresh coriander: ¼ cup, finely chopped
  • Mustard oil: 1 to 2 tablespoons (essential for authentic flavor)
  • Pickle masala (Achaar ka masala): 1 tablespoon (mango or stuffed red chili pickle works best)
  • Lemon juice: 1 tablespoon
  • Carom seeds (Ajwain): ½ teaspoon
  • Nigella seeds (Kalonji): ½ teaspoon
  • Salt: To taste (keeping in mind the pickle masala also contains salt)
  • Water: 1-2 tablespoons (just enough to make the mixture slightly moist and crumbly)

For the Chokha (Spicy Mash)

  • Eggplant (Baingan/Brinjal): 1 large (the round roasting variety)
  • Tomatoes: 2 medium-sized
  • Potatoes: 2 medium (boiled and peeled)
  • Garlic: 4-5 cloves (can be roasted inside the eggplant)
  • Onion: 1 medium, finely chopped
  • Green chilies: 2, finely chopped
  • Ginger: ½ inch, finely minced
  • Fresh coriander: 2 tablespoons, chopped
  • Mustard oil (Raw): 1 tablespoon (adds the traditional pungent kick)
  • Salt: To taste

Steps

  1. Knead the Dough: In a large bowl, mix the whole wheat flour, crushed ajwain, salt, and ghee. Rub the ghee into the flour until it resembles breadcrumbs. Gradually add water and knead into a dough that is firmer than regular roti dough. Cover with a damp cloth and let it rest.
  2. Roast the Chokha Vegetables: Make a few slits in the eggplant and insert the unpeeled garlic cloves inside. Roast the eggplant and tomatoes directly over a gas burner or charcoal fire on medium heat. Turn them occasionally until the skins are completely charred and the insides are soft. Set aside to cool.
  3. Mix the Sattu Stuffing: In a mixing bowl, combine the sattu, chopped onions, minced garlic, ginger, green chilies, coriander, mustard oil, pickle masala, lemon juice, ajwain, kalonji, and salt. Sprinkle 1-2 tablespoons of water and mix well. The mixture should feel dry and crumbly but clump together if you squeeze it in your fist.
  4. Mash the Chokha: Once the roasted eggplant and tomatoes are cool enough to handle, peel off the charred skins (it's okay if a few black flecks remain). Mash them in a bowl along with the boiled potatoes. Stir in the raw mustard oil, chopped onions, raw green chilies, minced ginger, fresh coriander, and salt.
  5. Stuff the Littis: Divide the rested dough into lemon-sized balls. Press the edges of a dough ball with your thumbs to form a small cup or bowl shape (keep the center slightly thicker than the edges). Place 2 tablespoons of the sattu stuffing inside. Bring the edges together, seal tightly, and roll it back into a smooth, round ball. Repeat for all the dough.
  6. Roast the Littis: On a gas stove: Place a wire roasting rack (jali) over the burner. Roast the littis on low-medium flame, turning frequently to avoid burning, until they are golden brown and develop slight cracks. In an oven: Preheat to 200°C (400°F). Bake for 30-40 minutes, flipping them halfway, until browned and cooked through.
  7. Dip in Ghee and Serve: Take the hot roasted littis and gently press or crack the top using your thumb or a spoon. Dunk them directly into a bowl of melted ghee so the fat seeps into the sattu center. Serve immediately alongside the freshly made chokha.
